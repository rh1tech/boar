Boar is an old-style BBS that runs today. You connect with SSH or telnet, pick a handle, and you're in. There's private mail, message boards, live chat, a door game and a wall of one-liners, all in ANSI colour with CP437 block art.

How to call

SSH encrypted

ssh -p 2222 bbs@boar-bbs.rh1.tech

Any user name works: you sign in to the BBS itself after connecting. Once you have an account you can add your SSH key under Settings, and then this command logs you straight in.

Telnet

telnet boar-bbs.rh1.tech

Port 23. Telnet sends everything unencrypted, your password included, so use SSH when you can.

A BBS client

SyncTERM, NetRunner or any other ANSI-BBS client works. Add boar-bbs.rh1.tech, telnet on port 23 or SSH on port 2222, and pick option 2 (CP437) when the BBS asks about your terminal.

The first time SSH asks about the server's key, check that it's SHA256:xMvzbhi5k5OnnNhmTObOjBzk6nGVrIRYRJGDP4aPNuI (ED25519)

Call from your browser

The same Boar BBS, with your existing handle and password, in an 80×25 UTF-8 terminal. The connection is encrypted all the way to the BBS in St. Petersburg. On a narrow screen, scroll the terminal sideways. For ZMODEM transfers, use a dedicated BBS client.

Not connected

Your first call

  1. Choose your terminal: 1 for UTF-8 (macOS Terminal, iTerm2, PuTTY), 2 for CP437 (SyncTERM and other BBS clients), 3 for plain ASCII.
  2. Type NEW at the handle prompt, then choose a handle and a password of at least 8 characters.
  3. Until a sysop approves the account you can read the boards and the news and mail a sysop. Once you're approved, everything else opens up.
  4. Use a window at least 80 columns wide. Long output pauses at -- more --.

What's inside

Mail
Private messages with threads, quoting and search. One message can go to several callers.
Boards
Public message boards, with a scan for everything new since your last call.
Chat
Live chat rooms, and one-line pages to anyone who's online.
Doors
Boar Hunt, a small door game.
The wall
One-liners shown at login, and news from the sysop.
Email
If you want it, a notice or a copy of new mail, sent only to an address you've verified.

FidoNet

Boar participates in FidoNet, the store-and-forward network BBSes have used since the 1980s. Our setup connects two locations: one shared BBS in St. Petersburg and a mail relay in Greece.

2:5030/1651
St. Petersburg, Russia — the main node and Boar BBS at boar-bbs.rh1.tech. Read echomail, use netmail and browse file echoes here.
2:410/51
Greece — our home-server mail relay at boar.rh1.tech. It exchanges mail with the Greek uplink and forwards it to the St. Petersburg BBS.

International echoes are already available. The Russian uplink is connected; additional Russian discussion-echo subscriptions are being configured. File echoes (nodelists and network files) are separate from discussion echoes. Mail travels on scheduled connections, so delivery is not instant.

House rules

Be decent to other callers. No spam, no advertising, and no attempts to break the system. A sysop can lock accounts that don't play along.

The BBS keeps your handle, a hash of your password, your messages and a log of logins. This page sets no cookies and loads no third-party resources. The browser terminal connects only when you choose Connect and uses the same BBS login and logging as SSH.

Boar BBS is free software. Questions? Call the BBS and mail a sysop.
